1. Getting Set Up

Everything starts from one page. Bookmark it — you won't need any other links.

Open ADV Media Teams
  1. Sign in with Google. Use whichever Google account you check regularly.
  2. Wait for approval. First time in, you'll see a "waiting on approval" screen. Dustin or Aracely have to approve you. You'll get in once they do — no need to sign in again, just check back.
  3. Pick your contract. Once approved, you'll see cards for CISD, AISD, and others. Tap the one you're working.
Do this before Thursday. If you wait until game day to sign in and you're not approved yet, you won't be able to clock in.

2. Your Profile & Photo

Open the Profile tab and fill it out completely. This takes two minutes and it drives everything else.

  • Legal name — as it appears on your ID. This is how the system matches you to shifts and invoices, so get it right.
  • Crew handle — what people actually call you. Important if someone else on crew shares your first name.
  • Skills / positions — what you're qualified for.
  • Phone — internal only, never shown on your ID card.
  • Emergency contact — name and number. Admin-only. We need this if something happens on site.
  • Photo — see below. This one matters most.

Your photo — read this part

Your photo is what gets you through the gate. Security compares your face to the ID card on your phone. A bad photo means you get held up at the gate, or turned away.
  • Face clearly visible, looking at the camera
  • Good light — no backlighting, no heavy shadow
  • Plain background if you can
  • No sunglasses, no hat brim over your eyes
  • Head and shoulders, like a work badge — not a full-body shot

Think driver's license, not Instagram. If a stranger couldn't match the photo to your face in three seconds, retake it.

3. Digital ID Card — Gate Access

The ID Card tab is your credential. Pull it up on your phone at the gate.

It shows your photo, your name, and the position you're working today — pulled live from the schedule. It updates on its own; there's nothing to request or print.

If you're not on today's schedule, the card shows "Not Scheduled Today" and security will turn you away. That's working as intended — but if you believe you are scheduled and it says otherwise, contact Dustin before you head to the venue, not when you're standing at the gate.

Screenshot it before you leave. Stadium cell service is unreliable and the card needs a connection to load. A screenshot always works.

4. Picking Up & Dropping Shifts

The Shifts tab shows every event and every position. Open positions say "Open." Yours show a Drop button.

Picking up a shift

Find an open position and tap Pick Up. It's yours immediately — first come, first served. If two people tap at the same time, the second person gets a message that it was just taken.

Dropping a shift

Tap Drop on a shift you can't work. You'll be asked for a reason — that's required, and it's logged.

Drop early. The moment you know you can't make it, drop it. A shift dropped a week out gets filled easily. One dropped the morning of leaves us scrambling, and it's noticed.

Dropping puts the position back on the board for someone else. It does not notify anyone automatically — if it's last minute, text Dustin or Aracely directly too.

5. Game Day — Start to Finish

The short version of what a normal event looks like:

  1. 1Before you leave: check the Today tab for call time, weather, and who else is working. Screenshot your ID card.
  2. 2Arrive on site. Get through the gate with your ID card.
  3. 3Clock in — has to be done at the venue, see below.
  4. 4Check out any gear you're taking from the audio booth.
  5. 5Pre-shift checklist for your position.
  6. 6Work the show.
  7. 7Post-shift checklist during wrap.
  8. 8Return all gear and submit an equipment report if anything's wrong.
  9. 9Clock out.
  10. 10Submit your invoice — same night is best.

6. Clocking In & Out

Open the Time Clock from the home page, pick the event, pick your position, and tap Clock In.

You must physically be at the venue. The app checks your location and only allows clock-in within about 500 feet. You can't clock in from the parking lot down the road, from home, or on the drive over. Allow location access when your phone asks — if you deny it, you can't clock in at all.

You only need to allow location at the moment you clock in. The app doesn't track you during your shift or afterward. "Allow Once" or "While Using App" both work fine.

Clock out when you're actually done — after wrap, not when the game clock hits zero. Your hours come from this.

7. Broadcast Checklist

Open the Checklist from the home page. Pick your position and work through the list. No login needed.

Every position has its own Pre-Shift and Post-Shift list — Camera Lead, Camera Op, Replay, Clips, Shader, Audio A1, A2, Utility, Director, Producer, PM, EIC.

  • Check items off as you go. It saves automatically — there's no submit button.
  • Signal Up when your position is ready. The EIC sees it on the dashboard, so nobody has to come find you.
  • Request Help if you're stuck. It flags your position red on the dashboard with your note. Use it — that's what it's for, and it's faster than hunting someone down mid-show.

The Dashboard view shows every position's status live, so you can see whether the rest of the crew is ready.

8. Equipment Checkout & Reports

Checking gear out of the audio booth

There's an iPad in the audio booth. Anything you take out of that booth gets checked out — mics, beltpacks, headsets, batteries, cables, the console, all of it.

  1. Tap your name
  2. Tap what you're taking
  3. Tap it again when you bring it back

Takes about five seconds. It exists so that when a mic goes missing, we know who had it last instead of guessing.

Mics and wireless comms need a battery checked out too. The iPad will remind you. Grab one every time.
Mic stands: do not go down to the mezzanine. Tap Request on the iPad and one will be brought up to you.

If you're taking something that isn't on the list, use "Check out something else" and type what it is. Nothing should leave the booth unlogged.

Equipment reports

After your shift, open the Equipment tab in the crew app and submit a report. Takes thirty seconds.

  • All Good — nothing to report
  • Minor Issue — noted, still usable
  • Needs Attention — repair or replace before next use

Be specific in the notes. "Camera 2 tripod head slips under load" is useful. "Tripod weird" is not.

Report it even if you worked around it. If you jiggled a cable to make it work, that's exactly what we need to know — otherwise the next person hits it cold during a live show.

9. Getting Paid — Submitting Your Invoice

You get paid from the invoice you submit. No invoice, no payment — it doesn't happen automatically from your clock-in.

How to submit

  1. Open the Time Clock from the home page
  2. Tap the Invoice tab
  3. Pick the event
  4. Fill in your name, position, hours, and rate — the total calculates itself
  5. Add notes if anything needs explaining (extra hours, unusual circumstances)
  6. Tap Submit
Submit the same night, or the next morning at the latest. Hours get fuzzy fast, and late invoices hold up the whole payment batch — not just yours.

Where to find your invoices

The My Invoices tab in the crew app shows everything you've submitted. Check there before asking whether one went through.

You can delete one you submitted by mistake. A permanent copy is kept on the admin side regardless, so deleting yours doesn't erase the record — if you need something corrected, say so rather than deleting and resubmitting.
