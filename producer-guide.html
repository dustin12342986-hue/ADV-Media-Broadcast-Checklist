What it does

The Run of Show tab holds the full script for a game. One person — the producer — drives it, and everyone else's screen follows theirs. When you move to the next cue, the whole crew moves with you.

That's the part worth understanding before you touch anything. This isn't a document each person scrolls on their own. It's one shared position, and you're holding it.

Before the show

Open ADV Media Teams and tap the Run of Show tab.

  1. Pick the game from the dropdown at the top. It opens on today's game, or tomorrow's if there's nothing today.
  2. Read through the cues. Scroll the whole thing once. This is when you catch a blank announcement or a name nobody filled in — not at 6:40 with a hot mic.
  3. Fix anything wrong now. See Editing cues.

Taking the show

Tap Make me producer at the bottom of the screen. If a passcode is set you'll see a lock and a four-digit keypad.

Once you have it, a red bar sits at the top of the tab:

● YOU ARE PRODUCER

Everyone else sees "[your name] is the producer" instead. If someone else is already holding it, your button reads Producer: Jasper Kane — tap to take over, and taking it needs the passcode.

Only the producer can drive. Next, Prev, play, speed and the re-cue button all require it. If a camera op taps Next, they get asked for the passcode — they can't move the crew's script by accident.

Tap the red bar again to release it when you're done, so the next show starts clean.

Auto-scroll

The row above the transport is a teleprompter. Press ▶ and the page creeps down on its own, on every crew member's phone at once.

  • Cues/min is the speed. At 2.0 a new cue reaches the top every 30 seconds. At 4.0, every 15.
  • − and + change it in half steps, 0.5 to 12.
  • Changing speed mid-run won't jump the page. It picks up from where it is.
  • Pressing ▶ from a stop starts at the cue you're calling, not wherever the page is sitting.
It's a pace, not a schedule. Auto-scroll ignores the clock times in the script and just moves at a steady rate. It's most useful through the quarters, where cues are "1st qtr" with no fixed time. In pregame, where everything has a real time, hitting Next ▶ as each cue happens is more accurate.

You can also just drag the page with your thumb. While you're the producer, the crew follows your drag. Let go and wherever you stopped becomes the shared position.

What the crew sees

Their screen follows yours. But they can look away without breaking anything:

  1. They touch their screen — the tally goes LIVE and following stops.
  2. They scroll wherever they want. Their screen stays put.
  3. Ten seconds after they stop touching it, it eases back to your position on its own.

They can also tap the grey tally, or the amber "Following paused — tap to catch up" bar, to come back immediately.

You're exempt. As producer you never get pulled anywhere. Your screen is the one everyone else is being pulled to.

Reading a cue card

Each line on a cue is colour-coded by department, so people can find their own without reading the whole card.

Announcer Screen Ribbon Audio Note
  • Announcer is word-for-word read copy, in a blue-ruled block. Bigger type on the live cue.
  • Note is amber because it's a warning — "English version", "may need on-field microphones", "read him last".

Editing cues

Admin only. Tap Edit at the top right.

  • Tap any cue to open it — section, time, duration, event, read copy, screen, ribbon, audio, notes.
  • ↑ ↓ reorder. ✕ deletes, with a confirm.
  • + Add Cue puts a new one at the end.
  • Reload from script throws away every edit and restores the version built into the app. Use it when a revised script comes in.
  • Set a producer passcode — four digits. The button shows the current code so you can look it up. Blank removes it.
Everyone needs the passcode, including admins. There's no bypass. Keep it somewhere you can find it, or read it off this screen before the show.

Edits save to everyone instantly. Reordering mid-show follows the live cue, so the cue you're calling stays the cue you're calling.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
Buttons ask for a passcode Someone else holds the show, or you released it. Tap the producer button and enter the code.
Crew are on the wrong cue Check the red bar says YOU ARE PRODUCER. If it names someone else, they're driving.
Page scrolled away from you Tap ⤒ to snap back to the cue you're calling.
Auto-scroll is drifting off pace Hit Next ▶ on the current cue. That re-anchors it and keeps running.
Wrong script for the game Check the dropdown at the top — it may be on the wrong date.
Cues look out of date Edit → Reload from script.
If the app stops responding mid-show, call it off the paper script. The run of show is a tool, not the show. Don't spend a live quarter troubleshooting a phone.
