SMPTE fiber — read this first

SMPTE fiber can never get wet on the ends. Whether they are covered or not, they must never be dragged through wet turf or puddles. It will ruin them instantly.

It's glass, not copper. A drop of water or a speck of grit on the end face stops light getting through — the camera still powers up, and you get no picture. There is no partial failure and no field repair. A ruined end means a dead position.

Striking a run

  1. Unplug the SMPTE from the JBT.
  2. Cap the end straight away.
  3. Plastic sleeve bag over the capped end. The cap alone is not a water seal.
  4. Same on the other end. Both ends, every time.
The two ends never touch the ground. Not on wet turf, not on dry concrete, not "just for a second" while you coil. Grit does the same damage as water, it just takes an extra show to show up.

Use reels for every camera. A reel keeps the ends off the ground by design instead of by everyone remembering. That's the fix — the rule above depends on people, the reel doesn't.

If an end does get wet or dropped: don't plug it in to see if it still works. Tell the EIC or A1, tag the cable, and use a spare. Mating a dirty end can push the contamination into the other connector and take out two ends instead of one.

Strike & secure

This is the part that stops a repeat of a camera being moved before we arrived. It costs ten minutes at strike and saves an hour of troubleshooting blind at 5pm.

  • De-rig every camera to the cage. A camera built on a mount is a camera someone can pick up. In the cage, that isn't possible.
  • Cap and bag both SMPTE ends before anything else — see SMPTE fiber. Ends never touch the ground.
  • Onto reels, not coiled on the turf.
  • Seal every SMPTE mate with a numbered seal or numbered zip tie. Write the numbers down.
  • Seal the case latches. One seal on a case beats checking six items inside it.
  • Photograph each position as you leave it.
  • Wireless goes home. Handhelds, the lav pack and ref headset, receivers, batteries. Small, expensive, and football barely uses them — they shouldn't sleep in someone else's building.

On arrival, before anything else: read the seal numbers against the list. A broken seal tells you what was touched before you start troubleshooting, instead of after.

If a seal is broken or gear has moved: photograph it before you touch anything, then tell the EIC or PM. The photo pair — how we left it, how we found it — is what makes this a conversation with the district instead of a complaint.
The cage only protects what's in it. Cable runs to camera positions can't be caged, which is exactly what got moved. Seals cover what the cage can't.
