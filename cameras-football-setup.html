SMPTE fiber — read this first

SMPTE fiber can never get wet on the ends. Whether they are covered or not, they must never be dragged through wet turf or puddles. It will ruin them instantly.

It's glass, not copper. A drop of water or a speck of grit on the end face stops light getting through — the camera still powers up, and you get no picture. There is no partial failure and no field repair. A ruined end means a dead position.

How to clean an end

Watch this before you clean one for the first time. The plug and the socket are cleaned differently, and the alignment sleeve stays in place on the plug — that's the step people get wrong.

Connecting and disconnecting

A connector that won't seat is almost never fixed by pushing harder. If it fights you, stop and inspect it.

Striking a run

  1. Unplug the SMPTE from the JBT.
  2. Cap the end straight away.
  3. Plastic sleeve bag over the capped end. The cap alone is not a water seal.
  4. Same on the other end. Both ends, every time.
The two ends never touch the ground. Not on wet turf, not on dry concrete, not "just for a second" while you coil. Grit does the same damage as water, it just takes an extra show to show up.

There are no reels yet. Everything is loose cable, which means the rule above depends entirely on people remembering it. Cap it, bag it, and put it down on something — a case lid, a bag, anything but the ground.

If an end does get wet or dropped: don't plug it in to see if it still works. Tell the EIC or A1, tag the cable, and use a spare. Mating a dirty end can push the contamination into the other connector and take out two ends instead of one.

In every camera case

You collect your case from the Camera Lead and hand it back to them. Packing it, the compartment layout, the inventory check and the QC tie are the Lead's job, not yours. If something is missing or damaged, tell them rather than sorting it yourself — they are tracking it.

Same in all four, so nobody has to remember which case has what:

  • Converter
  • Muffed headset
  • Weather gear — lives in the case, not the shop. It rains when it rains.
  • Sandbag — the one that matches that position's tripod
  • Power supply
  • Focus and zoom handles

Cameras 1, 2 and 3 also take an attaching viewfinder.

Camera 5 is the spare. It stays cased and comes to every game. The night you need it is the night you won't have time to go get it.

Strike

Your part is the camera and its run. Everything else is the Camera Lead's.

  • Unplug the SMPTE from the JBT, cap the end, plastic sleeve bag over the cap. Same on the other end — see SMPTE fiber.
  • Ends never touch the ground. Not wet turf, not dry concrete, not for a second while you coil.
  • Coil over-under. No reels yet, so coil it properly and keep the ends off the ground.

Coiling the same direction every loop builds twist into the jacket, and twist is what kinks a run and eventually breaks the fibre.

  • Break the camera down — tripod, sandbag, weather gear, handles, headset, converter, power supply — and put it back in its case.
  • Hand the case back to the Camera Lead. They check it against the inventory and seal it. Do not seal it yourself.
  • Tell the Lead about anything damaged or not working. Even if it still worked tonight.
